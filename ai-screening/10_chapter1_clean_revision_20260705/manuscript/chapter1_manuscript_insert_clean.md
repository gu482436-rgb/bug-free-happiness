## Computational Workflow

We established a computational screening pipeline to identify candidate antiviral peptides from fish bone type I collagen. Protein sequences (15 COL1A1/COL1A2 entries from eight aquaculture and capture-fishery fish species) were retrieved from UniProt and subjected to in silico digestion using eight protease cleavage rules: six strict-site rules (trypsin, Lys-C, Arg-C, Glu-C, Asp-N, and Lys-N) and two simplified screening rules (chymotrypsin and pepsin, applied as approximate cleavage models). Digestion was performed with zero and one missed cleavages. Resulting peptide fragments were filtered to 5-30 amino acids in length, molecular weight below 3,000 Da, and exclusion of sequences containing non-standard amino acid residues. After global deduplication, the expanded V2 library comprised 4,515 unique candidate peptides, a 3.2-fold increase over the baseline V1 library of 1,408 peptides derived previously from three species and three enzyme rules. All V1 peptides were recovered within the V2 library, with no V1-unique peptides remaining.

## Candidate Prioritization

Candidate antiviral peptide probability was predicted using a Random Forest classifier and an XGBoost classifier, both trained on a balanced dataset of 2,350 known antiviral and non-antiviral peptides with 433 sequence-derived features, including amino acid composition, dipeptide composition, and physicochemical descriptors. The two models achieved mean five-fold cross-validation accuracies of 0.868 (Random Forest) and 0.856 (XGBoost). The top 100 peptides ranked by mean prediction probability were submitted to three external computational validation tools: AI4AVP for antiviral peptide feature prediction (41/100 returned Positive), ToxinPred for toxicity-risk screening (100/100 returned Non-Toxin), and AllerCatPro for allergenicity evidence screening (40/100 returned "no evidence" of allergen epitope matches). Twelve peptides met all four computational criteria simultaneously: mean ML probability >= 0.85, AI4AVP Positive, ToxinPred Non-Toxin, and AllerCatPro "no evidence". From these, five algorithmically top-ranked peptides were selected as the V2 experiment-priority candidate set: NTQIENL, EVDATVKSL, NTQIDNL, TLTQTVENIR, and MNDRDNEVDATLKTL. Each peptide's occurrence and enzyme assignment were supported by the recorded in silico digestion outputs.

## Evidence Boundary and Limitations

All results presented here derive from computational screening and prediction tools. No peptide has been synthesized, and no experimental antiviral activity against norovirus or any other virus has been demonstrated. The following limitations therefore apply. First, the RF and XGBoost models are general antiviral peptide classifiers and do not target norovirus specifically; a positive prediction indicates AVP-like sequence features, not anti-noroviral efficacy. Second, AI4AVP, ToxinPred, and AllerCatPro are external web-based prediction tools whose training data, algorithm versions, and update schedules are outside our control. Their outputs are prediction-level evidence, not experimental validation. "Non-Toxin" does not establish safety, and "no evidence" of allergenicity does not confirm non-allergenicity. Third, four of the five V2 algorithmic Top5 candidates are generated by the pepsin simplified screening rule (F/L/W/Y cleavage), which is an in silico approximation and does not capture pH-dependent, structure-dependent, or process-dependent pepsin cleavage behavior. Fourth, the five candidate peptides are short (7-15 amino acids) and, although verified as products of project fish collagen in silico digestion, may also occur in non-collagen proteins. They should therefore be described as fish collagen-derived candidate peptides for validation, not as collagen-specific peptides. Finally, molecular docking results against the norovirus VP1 P domain are computational pose predictions; scrambled-sequence control peptides yielded comparable scores in the current analysis, indicating that docking alone does not establish sequence-specific binding.

## Next Experimental Validation

The V2 algorithmic Top5 should be treated as a computationally prioritized shortlist for experimental validation. A practical validation sequence would begin with solid-phase synthesis of the Top3 candidates (NTQIENL, EVDATVKSL, and NTQIDNL), followed by identity and purity confirmation by HPLC and mass spectrometry. Antiviral activity should then be assessed using murine norovirus (MNV) as a surrogate model in cell culture, with appropriate positive controls and scrambled-sequence negative controls. A norovirus virus-like particle (NoV-VLP) binding assay, such as surface plasmon resonance, isothermal titration calorimetry, or ELISA, should be used to determine whether candidate peptides physically interact with the viral capsid. In parallel, in vitro cytotoxicity and hemolysis assays should be performed to test the computational non-toxicity and non-hemolysis predictions. If candidates are intended for food-contact applications, peptide stability under relevant pH, temperature, protease-exposure, and matrix conditions should also be characterized. The salmon collagen-derived peptide NSQIENL may be retained only as a salmon-context synthesis-priority hypothesis: it exists as an independent V2 peptide-library entry (V2PEP003364) and has V2 RF/XGBoost prediction scores, but it was not selected into the V2 Top100, Top20, or algorithmic Top5 and therefore lacks V2 external validation. None of these candidates should be described as validated antiviral peptides, safe for human consumption, or proven food preservatives until the corresponding experimental evidence is obtained.
